# ICP, Buying Signals & Scoring

Reference this file whenever running the scout (`SKILL.md` Section 4). Re-check candidates against these tables explicitly — don't approximate from memory.

---

## Ideal Company Profile

Prioritise companies meeting **most** of these criteria (not all — treat as a weighted fit, not a checklist gate):

- 15–300 employees
- Seed to Series C
- Profitable or clearly on a path to profitability at the growth stage
- Recently funded
- Recently launched a product
- Expanding internationally
- Hiring across Growth, Marketing, Operations, or Business Development
- Scaling rapidly (headcount, revenue, or market footprint)

**Target industries**: AI, FinTech, SaaS, Enterprise Software, B2B Technology, Infrastructure/API, Developer Tools, Digital Assets, Marketplaces, high-growth D2C.

A company with 5 of 8 criteria and a strong signal beats a company with 8 of 8 and no signal — fit alone never justifies a recommendation without Section 2 below.

---

## Buying Signals (the actual filter)

A candidate needs **at least one** dated, verifiable signal to qualify at all. More signals = higher priority. Signals to prioritise:

- Raised funding
- Launched a product
- Entered a new market
- Rebranded
- Hired a GTM leader (CMO, CGO, VP Growth/Marketing/Sales)
- Opened multiple commercial roles at once
- Expanded the team rapidly
- Announced a strategic partnership
- Publicly mentioned AI transformation initiatives
- Publicly mentioned operational scaling challenges (earnings calls, founder interviews, job descriptions hinting at process gaps)

If you can only find "this looks like a good ICP fit" and no dated signal from the list above, it does not go on the shortlist — flag it as a watch-list candidate instead if it's genuinely promising, but don't count it toward the daily 10.

## Problem → Signal Mapping

Use this to write the "business challenges Meiseki could solve" field with real specificity instead of generic language.

| Trigger signal | Likely underlying problem | Meiseki service angle |
|---|---|---|
| Just raised a round | Needs to scale GTM fast without breaking what worked at smaller scale; founder bottleneck risk as team grows | Fractional CGO/CMO, GTM strategy, RevOps |
| Recently launched product | Positioning/ICP may still be unproven; conversion and messaging not yet optimized | Product Marketing, Go-to-Market, Market Research |
| Entering a new market/geo | No local competitive intelligence, unproven positioning, partnership gaps | Competitive Intelligence, Market Research, Partnership Strategy |
| Hiring first GTM/growth leader | Founder has been doing GTM ad hoc; needs an operating system before (or instead of) a full-time hire | Fractional CGO, interim leadership bridge |
| Opened many commercial roles at once | Scaling BD/sales without a defined process; onboarding and pipeline chaos likely | RevOps, Business Development process design |
| Rapid headcount growth generally | Cross-functional inefficiencies, manual processes not built for current scale | Operations Partner, Process Optimisation, AI Workflow Design |
| Announced a partnership | Actively building partnership motion — may need a repeatable partnership strategy, not one-off deals | Partnership Strategy, Business Development |
| Public AI-transformation mention | Wants AI adoption but likely lacks a workflow-level implementation plan | AI Workflow Design & Implementation |
| Founder interview mentions "scaling pains" / "wearing too many hats" | Founder bottleneck, no operating system | Fractional COO-style Operations Partner, Executive Decision Support |

## Scoring Rubric

Score every shortlisted opportunity 1–10 on both axes before ranking.

**Urgency (1–10)** — how time-boxed is the need?
- 8–10: Signal happened in the last 2–4 weeks, active hiring for the exact role Meiseki could fill on a fractional basis, or a funding round explicitly earmarked for GTM/expansion.
- 5–7: Signal happened in the last 1–3 months, clear need but no explicit deadline pressure.
- 1–4: Signal is older or indirect (e.g. general "high-growth" reputation without a recent dated event).

**Budget fit (1–10)** — can they plausibly afford a fractional engagement?
- 8–10: Recently funded (especially Series A+) with a round large enough to cover fractional/consulting spend, or already profitable at scale.
- 5–7: Funded but early/small round, or profitable but lean — plausible but needs a lower-cost entry point (e.g. a scoped project vs. ongoing fractional retainer).
- 1–4: Pre-seed/bootstrapped with no funding signal and no clear revenue — deprioritise even with a strong pain signal.

Opportunities scoring below ~5 on **both** axes should not make the daily top 10 — they belong on a watch list at most.

## Referral Partner Fit (separate lens)

Referral partners aren't scored on urgency/budget the same way — score them on **reach into the ICP** and **relationship warmth**:

- VC Platform/Operating Partners: high reach (see the whole portfolio), warmth depends on whether they run a public platform program vs. purely internal support.
- Startup studios: high reach into pre-seed/seed, often looking for outside operators to plug into portfolio companies.
- Fractional executives (non-competing functions, e.g. fractional CFOs/CTOs): high warmth, moderate reach — natural two-way referral relationship.
- Product/Dev/Design agencies: high reach into growth-stage clients who lack strategy but have execution budget — strong complementary-service fit.
- Executive recruiters: see hiring intent before it's public (e.g. before a GTM leader search is announced) — high-value early-signal source, not just a referral channel.
